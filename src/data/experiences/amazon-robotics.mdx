## Scope

Amazon Robotics operates two distinct fleet architectures. Structured floor robots follow predetermined paths on fixed grids — the classic drive-unit-under-pod model that powers the majority of fulfillment volume. Autonomous floor robots navigate freely through open warehouse space shared with human workers, requiring computed paths rather than predefined routes. My work focuses on the autonomous floor navigation stack: generating collision-free paths with waypoints and execution envelopes, enforcing traffic policies to manage congestion, and estimating travel times used by downstream systems for task allocation and scheduling. Earlier work also touched structured floor systems through shared data infrastructure that serves both fleet types.

## Scale

The autonomous floor grew from 10 sites and 420 robots to over 36 sites and 4,700 robots in the past year. Path planning requests scaled from 90,000 to 900,000 per day over the same period. Supporting data pipelines ingest over 455 million path records daily across both fleet types, serving 9 downstream consumers with sub-500ms data freshness. These robots operate in the same physical space as human workers, so correctness and safety are non-negotiable. Floor layouts are no static — temporary restricted zones, shifting storage configurations, and varying robot load states all require the navigation stack to adapt continuously without manual intervention.


## Technical Depth

The work spans computational geometry, graph algorithms, and distributed systems. Physical warehouse maps get decomposed into navigable spatial representations — triangulated meshes, distance fields for clearance enforcement, and sparse graphs for efficient travel time estimation. Search algorithms produce optimal paths under real-time latency constraints while respecting clearance requirements, traffic policies, and dynamic obstacles. Travel time estimation operates on precomputed graph structures to deliver sub-second responses where previous approaches took minutes.
